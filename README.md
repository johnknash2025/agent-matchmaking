# Agent Matchmaking (concept)

> Working name. **Concept only: there is no app and no code yet.** This page describes the idea so it can be discussed.

**One line:** each person has a personal AI agent that knows what they are looking for in a partner; agents talk to each other first, and two people are introduced only when both agents see a good match and both people agree.

## The problem
Matchmaking apps ask people to judge strangers from photos and a short profile, and then to start many conversations that go nowhere. The work that matters (values, life plans, how two people would actually get along) happens late, after a lot of time and some hurt. Marriage-minded people in particular want fewer, better introductions.

## Why
Matchmaking has become painful, and many people give up. Most apps turn people into a short list of attributes (income, height, photos) and lose what actually draws two people together: how a conversation flows, how values fit, what it feels like to be together. Their business model also rewards keeping people in the app, not helping them leave it as a couple.

## Roles: one matchmaker, two gatekeepers
| Role | Whose side | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Matchmaker agent** (one, neutral) | The relationship between the two people | Looks at what each gatekeeper has allowed it to see and asks: could these two build a story together? It never helps one side win. |
| **Each person's own local agent** (already theirs) | The person | A gatekeeper for what the person really thinks. It keeps that on the person's own device and passes on only what the person allows. If the person says no, it stops. |
| **The people** | | Meet, decide, marry, and decide whether to have children. |

The agents support; humans decide. This is not two agents negotiating against each other, and the matchmaker never holds everyone's private thoughts in one place.

## What the matchmaker is rewarded for
- **Both people, not one.** Success is when both would say it was good, and when the relationship lasts. A match that is good for only one side is a failure.
- **Consent is a hard line, not a weight.** However good the matchmaker thinks a match is, if either person says no, it does not go ahead. No score can override that.
- **Family and children only when both want it.** If both want children, the matchmaker helps that go well (timing, housing, work). If they do not, that is a good story too.
- **Society's goals stay outside the system.** More good relationships may help society carry on, but that is a hoped-for result, not the matchmaker's reward.

### Why not reward population or birth rates directly
- Nobody would trust an agent with their real thoughts if it served a goal other than them.
- Choosing people by whether they are likely to have children is a worse kind of reducing people to attributes, and excludes people who do not want or cannot have children, and same-sex couples.
- Putting an outside goal above a person's own wishes is manipulation.
- "Society carrying on" cannot be measured, so an agent would chase stand-in numbers (marriages, births), the same trap as apps chasing time spent.

## The idea
1. **A personal agent per person.** It learns, through conversation, what the person values, what they will not compromise on, and how they like to communicate. Sensitive details stay with the person's own agent.
2. **Agents meet first.** Two agents compare what each person wants, step by step, sharing only what is needed at each step. Most mismatches end here, quietly, with no rejection felt by anyone.
3. **People decide.** When both agents see a good match, each person receives a short explanation of *why*, and decides whether to be introduced. Nothing is shared with the other person without consent.
4. **Real people, real meetings.** Each person is verified as a real human, and the goal is meeting in person. The agent helps arrange that; it does not replace the relationship.

## Principles
- **Consent at every step.** Sharing more information, being introduced, and meeting each need a yes from the person.
- **The agent never pretends to be the person** in conversations with another human. When an agent writes, it says so.
- **No engagement tricks.** Success is a good introduction, not time spent in the app.
- **Minimal data.** Share the least needed at each step; let people delete everything.

## Hard questions (not solved)
| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Bias in matching | Agents could repeat or amplify unfair preferences (for example by background or appearance). |
| Deception | People may describe themselves untruthfully to their own agent; verification only covers identity, not character. |
| Sensitive data | Values, health, finances and family plans are among the most personal data there is. |
| Safety | Protecting people from harassment, romance scams and stalking, before and after an introduction. |
| Regulation | Online dating and matchmaking services are regulated in Japan (age checks, notifications to the authorities); this needs legal review before any service. |
